Tom's Seafood Boil

A flexible seafood boil built for outdoor propane burners and family-style table service, with room to cook it mild, spicy, or mild with the heat added later.

Serves
12 big gathering style
Cook Setup
1 or 2 Pots outdoor burner friendly
Main Flavor
Mild or Spicy or spice later
Method
Seafood Boil drain and dump
how this one is built

One master recipe, then pick your path

This version is written as one core seafood boil recipe first so it reads cleanly on the page. From there, you can cook it fully mild, fully spicy, or keep the pot mild and let people add heat later with Cajun butter, hot sauce, or extra seasoning at the table.

If you have two outdoor turkey-fryer burners, split the ingredients between two pots and run one mild and one spicy side by side. If you only want one pot, the exact same order of cooking still works — just choose the flavor path you want and keep going.

Flavor path

Choose mild, spicy, or mild with spice added later. The main recipe stays the same; only the broth seasonings and finishing options change.

Pot setup

Use one large pot for a single flavor, or two pots if you want a mild side and a spicy side at the same time. Mussels belong only in the mild version.

Ingredients

Seafood and meat
  • 6 lb large shell-on shrimp, deveined
  • 4 to 5 lb large sea scallops
  • 5 lb crab legs
  • 3 lb littleneck clams, scrubbed
  • 2 lb mussels, scrubbed, for the mild version only
  • 2 lb andouille sausage, sliced into 1-inch rounds
  • 2 lb smoked kielbasa, sliced into 1-inch rounds
Vegetables and aromatics
  • 5 lb small Yukon Gold or red potatoes, halved
  • 12 ears corn, cut into 2 to 3-inch chunks
  • 2 lb green beans, trimmed, optional
  • 4 large yellow onions, quartered
  • 2 whole heads garlic, halved horizontally
  • 8 lemons, 6 for the broth and 2 for serving
  • 1 large bunch fresh parsley, chopped
  • 18 to 24 eggs, optional, added raw to the boil
Seasonings and finishing
  • Old Bay seasoning
  • Cajun seasoning
  • Cayenne pepper
  • 2 to 3 fresh chiles, halved, for the spicy version
  • 8 bay leaves
  • Kosher salt
  • 2 to 3 cups unsalted butter for finishing or table sauces

Directions

Mild broth

Use lemon, onion, garlic, bay leaves, kosher salt, and a lighter amount of Old Bay. This is the only version that gets the mussels.

Spicy broth

Use the same base plus more Old Bay, Cajun seasoning, cayenne, and fresh chiles. Skip the mussels here and let the broth carry the heat.

1. Build the broth. In a large outdoor boil pot, add about 3 gallons of water, 3 halved lemons, 2 quartered onions, 1 halved garlic head, and 4 bay leaves. For mild, season with about 2 tablespoons kosher salt and 1/4 cup Old Bay. For spicy, use about 2 tablespoons kosher salt, 1/2 cup Old Bay, 1/4 cup Cajun seasoning, 1 to 2 teaspoons cayenne, and 2 to 3 halved fresh chiles. Bring to a strong simmer for 10 to 15 minutes, then taste and adjust.
2. Add the potatoes first. Add the potatoes and simmer 10 to 12 minutes, until they are just starting to soften but are not fully done.
3. Add corn and sausage. Add the corn, andouille, and kielbasa. Simmer another 8 to 10 minutes.
4. Add the optional eggs and green beans. If using eggs, lower them in raw and simmer 10 to 12 minutes so they hard-boil in the seasoned broth. Add green beans in the last 5 to 6 minutes so they keep their color.
5. Add shellfish and crab. Add the clams to any version. Add mussels only if you are cooking the mild version. Simmer until the shells open, about 5 to 7 minutes, then add the crab legs and cook another 3 to 5 minutes to heat through. Discard any clams or mussels that do not open.
6. Finish with shrimp and scallops. Add the shrimp and scallops last. Simmer gently for 2 to 3 minutes, then turn off the heat, cover the pot, and let the seafood finish in the hot broth for 8 to 10 minutes.
7. Drain and dump for serving. Lift the basket if your pot has one, or drain using a large colander. Pour the boil out onto a table lined with a cloth and butcher paper. Finish with chopped parsley, lemon wedges, and warm butter sauces if using.

Notes and options

Mild with spice added later

If you want one pot that works for everyone, cook the full recipe as the mild version and serve extra heat on the table. Cajun butter, hot sauce, and a bowl of extra Cajun seasoning let each person push the spice where they want it.

Two-pot variation

To cook mild and spicy side by side, split the potatoes, corn, sausage, shrimp, scallops, crab, clams, eggs, and green beans evenly between two pots. Put all mussels into the mild pot only, then follow the same cooking order in both pots.

One-pot variation

To cook this as a single pot, keep the ingredient list the same and simply choose one broth path. Mild is the safest crowd option; spicy gives you the strongest built-in flavor.

Safety

  • Cook outdoors on a flat, stable surface with the burner away from anything flammable.
  • Do not overfill the pot; leave room for boiling, stirring, and draining.
  • Keep kids and pets away from the burners and hot liquid.
  • Use heat-safe gloves when lifting baskets, lids, or draining heavy pots.
  • Turn off the propane before moving equipment and keep a fire extinguisher nearby.
