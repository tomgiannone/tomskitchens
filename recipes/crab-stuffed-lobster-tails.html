Crab-Stuffed Lobster Tails

Butterflied lobster tails piled high with a lemony lump crab stuffing, baked under a golden panko crust and finished with garlic lemon butter. Steakhouse dinner, home kitchen.

          Tools

          • Sturdy kitchen shears
          • Sheet pan lined with foil or parchment
          • Mixing bowl for the stuffing
          • Small bowl for the topping
          • Small saucepan for the butter
          • Pastry brush
          • Microplane or zester
          • Instant-read thermometer

          Prep · about 25 minutes

          1. Heat the oven to 425°F and line a sheet pan with foil or parchment.

          2. Butterfly the tails. With kitchen shears, cut straight down the center of the top shell to the base of the tail fin. Don't cut through the fin. Gently spread the shell open with your thumbs.

          3. Piggyback the meat. Run your fingers under the meat to loosen it from the shell, leaving it attached at the tail end. Lift the meat up and rest it on top of the closed shell. Cut a shallow slit down the center of the meat to make a pocket for the stuffing.

            Tip: frozen tails thaw best overnight in the fridge. Pat them dry before you start.

          4. Season. Brush the meat with melted butter, then sprinkle lightly with salt and paprika.

          5. Make the stuffing. In a bowl, gently fold the crumbled crab cakes with the mayo, Dijon, lemon zest and juice, garlic, Old Bay, parsley, and panko. Fold, don't stir, so the lump crab stays in big pieces.

            Using raw crab cakes? Bake them at 400°F for about 10 minutes until just cooked through, then cool slightly and crumble.

          6. Stuff the tails. Mound about ⅓ cup of stuffing onto each tail, pressing it lightly into the slit and over the top of the meat.

          7. Add the topping. Stir the topping panko with the melted butter and a pinch of Old Bay, then sprinkle it evenly over the stuffing.

          Cook · oven at 425°F

          01 Bake
          • Bake at 425°F (220°C) for 12–15 minutes, until the lobster meat is opaque.
          • Plan on about 1 to 1½ minutes per ounce per tail. Bigger tails need a few extra minutes.
          02 Broil for the crust
          • Switch to broil for 1–2 minutes until the panko is deep golden.
          • Stay at the oven. The crust goes from golden to burnt fast.
          03 Garlic lemon butter
          • While the tails bake, melt the butter with the garlic over low heat for about 2 minutes. Don't let the garlic brown.
          • Off the heat, stir in the lemon juice and parsley.
          04 Doneness check
          • The thickest part of the lobster meat should read 140°F (60°C).
          • Pull them right at temp. Overcooked lobster turns rubbery.
          to finish

          Garnish with chopped parsley and a lemon wedge. Spoon some of the garlic lemon butter over each tail and serve the rest on the side for dipping. A loaded baked potato makes it a full steakhouse plate.

          Tips & pitfalls

          Shortcut stuffing

          Good lump crab cakes already have the binder and seasoning, so crumbling them saves a step. Go lighter on the Old Bay if yours are well seasoned.

          Don't overcook

          Use a thermometer and pull the tails at 140°F. The meat keeps cooking for a minute on the pan.

          Make ahead

          Mix the stuffing up to a day ahead. Tails can be butterflied and stuffed up to 4 hours ahead and refrigerated.

          One-oven timing

          Baked potatoes also go at 425°F. Start them about 65 minutes before dinner and put the tails in when the potatoes have 15 minutes left.

          Easy sides

          A loaded baked potato with butter, cheddar, bacon bits, and chives (the one in the photo). Roasted asparagus or a Caesar salad also work great.
