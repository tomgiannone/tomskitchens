Sausage, Apple & Sage Stuffing

All the cozy comfort of classic holiday stuffing, with a whole lot more flavor. Savory sage sausage, sweet apple, and tender onion simmer with fresh herbs and rich stock, then get folded into a fluffy, golden side that earns its spot next to the turkey. It starts with a boxed mix, so it's easy, but no one will ever guess.

Boxes
3
Serves

For the stuffing

    Fresh herbs

        Tools

        • Large pot or deep skillet with a lid
        • Wooden spoon for crumbling sausage
        • Cutting board & sharp knife
        • Fork for fluffing
        • Buttered 9×13 baking dish (optional, for baking)

        Stovetop · about 20 minutes

        1. Brown the sausage. In a large pot over medium-high heat, cook the sausage, breaking it into small crumbles, until browned, about 8 minutes. Move it to a plate and leave about 2 tbsp of the fat in the pot.

        2. Cook the onion and apple. Add 2 tbsp of the butter, the onion, and the apple. Cook until soft and lightly golden, 6 to 8 minutes. Add the garlic, sage, and thyme for the last minute.

        3. Add the liquid. Pour in the stock (hold back the extra) and the remaining butter. Bring it to a boil.

        4. Add the stuffing. Take the pot off the heat. Stir in the stuffing mix and the sausage, cover, and let it sit for 5 minutes.

        5. Fluff and adjust. Fluff with a fork and stir in the parsley and black pepper. If it seems dry, add the reserved stock a little at a time.

          Taste before adding any salt. The mix, the stock, and the sausage are all salty, so it usually doesn't need any.

        Optional · baked crispy top

        01 Set up
        • Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C) and butter a 9×13 baking dish.
        • Make the stuffing on the stove as above, but leave it slightly wet.
        02 Bake
        • Stir in the beaten egg so it holds together, then spread it in the dish.
        • Dot the top with butter and bake uncovered for 20–25 minutes, until golden and crisp on top.
        to finish

        Sprinkle with a little more fresh parsley and serve hot, straight from the pot or the baking dish.

        Tips & pitfalls

        Use low-sodium stock

        The mix and the sausage bring plenty of salt. Low-sodium stock keeps it from going too far.

        Hold back some liquid

        The apple and onion release moisture. Add the last ½ cup of stock only if it looks dry.

        Pick your sausage

        Sage breakfast sausage is the classic. Sweet Italian also works great.

        Make ahead

        Brown the sausage and cook the onion and apple the day before. Refrigerate, then warm them in the pot and pick up at step 3.

        Making 2 boxes?

        Use the box selector above. It scales everything to about two-thirds.
