Tom's Seafood Boil

A flexible seafood boil built for outdoor propane burners and family-style table service, with room to cook it mild, spicy, or mild with the heat added later.

This recipe reads as one shared seafood boil with two ways to cook it: a straightforward one-pot version, or a cleaner two-pot setup where mild and spicy cook side by side at the same time.

Ingredients

Seafood and meat
  • 6 lb large shell-on shrimp, deveined
  • 4 to 5 lb large sea scallops
  • 5 lb crab legs
  • 3 lb littleneck clams, scrubbed
  • 2 lb mussels, scrubbed, for the mild or spice-later version only
  • 2 lb andouille sausage, sliced into 1-inch rounds
  • 2 lb smoked kielbasa, sliced into 1-inch rounds
Vegetables and aromatics
  • 5 lb small Yukon Gold or red potatoes, halved
  • 12 ears corn, cut into 2 to 3-inch chunks
  • 2 lb green beans, trimmed, optional
  • 4 large yellow onions, quartered
  • 2 whole heads garlic, halved horizontally
  • 8 lemons, 6 for the broth and 2 for serving
  • 1 large bunch fresh parsley, chopped
  • 18 to 24 eggs, optional
Seasonings and finishing
  • Old Bay seasoning
  • Cajun seasoning
  • Cayenne pepper
  • 2 to 3 fresh chiles, halved, for the spicy version
  • 8 bay leaves
  • Kosher salt
  • 2 to 3 cups unsalted butter for finishing or table sauces

Directions

Mild broth

Use lemon, onion, garlic, bay leaves, kosher salt, and a lighter amount of Old Bay. This version stays family-friendly and is the only path that gets mussels.

Spicy broth

Use the same base, then push it harder with extra Old Bay, Cajun seasoning, cayenne, and fresh chiles. This pot skips the mussels and carries the heat directly in the broth.

Mild + spice later

Cook the broth as a mild version, then put the heat on the table with Cajun butter, hot sauce, and extra seasoning so each person can build their own plate.

1. Build the broth. In a large outdoor boil pot, add about 3 gallons of water, 3 halved lemons, 2 quartered onions, 1 halved garlic head, and 4 bay leaves. For mild or spice-later, season with about 2 tablespoons kosher salt and 1/4 cup Old Bay. For spicy, use about 2 tablespoons kosher salt, 1/2 cup Old Bay, 1/4 cup Cajun seasoning, 1 to 2 teaspoons cayenne, and 2 to 3 halved fresh chiles. Bring to a strong simmer for 10 to 15 minutes, then taste and adjust.
2. Add the potatoes first. Add the potatoes and simmer 10 to 12 minutes, until they are just starting to soften but are not fully done.
3. Add corn and sausage. Add the corn, andouille, and kielbasa. Simmer another 8 to 10 minutes.
4. Add eggs and green beans. If using eggs, lower them in raw and simmer 10 to 12 minutes so they hard-boil in the seasoned broth. Add green beans in the last 5 to 6 minutes so they keep their color.
5. Add shellfish and crab. Add the clams to any version. Add mussels only if you are cooking the mild or spice-later path. Simmer until the shells open, about 5 to 7 minutes, then add the crab legs and cook another 3 to 5 minutes to heat through. Discard any clams or mussels that do not open.
6. Finish with shrimp and scallops. Add the shrimp and scallops last. Simmer gently for 2 to 3 minutes, then turn off the heat, cover the pot, and let the seafood finish in the hot broth for 8 to 10 minutes.
7. Drain and dump for serving. Lift the basket if your pot has one, or drain using a large colander. Pour the boil out onto a table lined with a cloth and butcher paper. Finish with chopped parsley, lemon wedges, and warm butter sauces if using.
1. Split the full recipe into two matching lanes. Divide the potatoes, corn, sausage, shrimp, scallops, crab, clams, eggs, and green beans evenly between two pots. Put all mussels into the pot that will stay mild or be used for the spice-later path.
2. Build both broths at the same time. In each pot, add about 2 gallons of water, 2 halved lemons, 2 quartered onions, 1 halved garlic head, and 4 bay leaves. Season one pot as mild and the other as spicy. Keep both pots on the same timer so the cooking stays easy to track.
3. Run both pots in the same order. Add potatoes to both pots and simmer 10 to 12 minutes. Add corn and sausage to both pots and simmer another 8 to 10 minutes.
4. Add the middle ingredients together. Add eggs and green beans to both pots. Add clams to both pots, but add mussels only to the mild pot. Once the shells open, add crab legs to both pots and cook 3 to 5 minutes.
5. Finish both pots the same way. Add shrimp and scallops to each pot, simmer 2 to 3 minutes, then turn off the heat and let both pots sit covered 8 to 10 minutes.
6. Drain and serve side by side. Drain each pot separately and pour them onto the table in two clear lanes — one mild and one spicy — so people can choose the version they want without guesswork.

Notes and options

What changes when you use two pots

The ingredient list stays the same overall, but the work gets easier because both pots move through the same order together. The only real difference is that the mild pot gets the mussels and the spicy pot gets the extra heat.

Mild with spice added later

If you want one setup that keeps everyone happy, cook the broth in the mild range and let people add Cajun butter, hot sauce, or extra seasoning at the table.

Safety

  • Cook outdoors on a flat, stable surface with the burner away from anything flammable.
  • Do not overfill the pot; leave room for boiling, stirring, and draining.
  • Keep kids and pets away from the burners and hot liquid.
  • Use heat-safe gloves when lifting baskets, lids, or draining heavy pots.
  • Turn off the propane before moving equipment and keep a fire extinguisher nearby.
