Tom's Seafood Boil

A flexible seafood boil for outdoor burners and family-style table service, written so you can cook it mild, spicy, or mild with the heat added later — in one pot or two side-by-side.

Servings
12
Prep
25 min
Cook
45–55 min
Method
Boil 1 or 2 pots
Recipe scaled for 12 servings
Flavor path

Mild keeps the broth crowd-friendly and is the only version that includes mussels.

Pot setup

One pot keeps the whole recipe in one clean flow if you only have one burner.

How this recipe works

This page is built as one master seafood boil recipe first, then lets you switch the way it reads based on how you are actually cooking it. The ingredient list scales with servings, while the toggle controls change the broth description and the cooking flow you see below.

If you are making both mild and spicy at the same time, the two-pot version is written in a parallel way so both pots move together and are easier to manage.

        Flavor notes

        Mild broth

        Use lemon, onion, garlic, bay leaves, kosher salt, and a lighter hand with Old Bay. This version stays balanced and is the one that gets the mussels.

        Spicy broth

        Start with the same base, then push it harder with more Old Bay, Cajun seasoning, cayenne, and fresh chiles. Skip the mussels here and let the broth carry the heat.

        Mild broth with spice added later

        Build the pot as a mild boil, then let people add Cajun butter, hot sauce, or extra seasoning at the table so everyone can control the heat on their own plate.

        Cook — one pot

        01 Build the broth
        • Add water, lemon, onion, garlic, and bay leaves to the pot and bring it to a lively simmer for 10 to 15 minutes.
        • For mild, season with kosher salt and a lighter amount of Old Bay.
        • Taste the broth before you start loading the pot. It should taste bold now, because the potatoes, corn, and shellfish will mellow it out later.
        02 Start with the longest-cooking pieces
        • Add the potatoes first and simmer 10 to 12 minutes, until they are beginning to soften but are not fully done.
        • Add the corn plus both sausages and simmer another 8 to 10 minutes.
        03 Add the middle ingredients
        • Add eggs, if using, early enough that they can boil in the broth for about 10 to 12 minutes.
        • Add green beans in the last 5 to 6 minutes so they stay bright and do not turn mushy.
        • Add clams to the pot and add mussels only if you are making the mild version.
        • Once the shells open, add the crab legs and heat them through for another 3 to 5 minutes.
        04 Finish gently
        • Add the shrimp and scallops last so they do not overcook.
        • Simmer gently for 2 to 3 minutes, then turn off the heat, cover the pot, and let the seafood finish in the hot broth for 8 to 10 minutes.
        • Discard any clams or mussels that do not open.
        05 Drain and dump for serving
        • Lift the basket if your pot has one, or drain the boil carefully with a large colander.
        • Pour everything onto a paper-lined table or into large trays.
        • Finish with chopped parsley, lemon wedges, and warm butter on the side.

        Cook — two pots at once

        01 Split the recipe into two lanes
        • Divide the potatoes, corn, sausage, shrimp, scallops, crab, clams, eggs, and green beans as evenly as you can between the two pots.
        • Keep all mussels in the mild pot only.
        • This setup works best when both pots move in the same order and on roughly the same timer.
        02 Build both broths at the same time
        • Add water, lemon, onion, garlic, and bay leaves to both pots and bring both to a simmer together.
        • Season one pot mild and the other spicy, then let both broths simmer 10 to 15 minutes before adding the first ingredients.
        • Taste both broths and adjust before moving on, because it is much easier to fix seasoning now than later.
        03 Run the first wave in both pots
        • Add potatoes to both pots and simmer 10 to 12 minutes.
        • Add corn and sausage to both pots and simmer another 8 to 10 minutes.
        • Keeping both pots on the same sequence makes the whole cook much easier to track.
        04 Add the middle ingredients together
        • Add eggs and green beans to both pots as needed.
        • Add clams to both pots, but add mussels only to the mild pot.
        • Once shells open, add crab legs to both pots and heat through for 3 to 5 minutes.
        05 Finish both pots the same way
        • Add shrimp and scallops to both pots.
        • Simmer 2 to 3 minutes, then turn off both burners and let the pots sit covered for 8 to 10 minutes.
        • Discard any clams or mussels that do not open.
        06 Serve side by side
        • Drain each pot separately.
        • Dump the mild and spicy boils in two clear lanes on the table so people can choose without guessing.
        • Finish both with chopped parsley, lemon wedges, and butter sauces, keeping hotter sauces near the spicy side.

        Notes & tips

        Mild is the safest crowd option

        If you are feeding a mixed group, the mild path or the mild-plus-spice-later path is usually the easiest way to keep everyone happy.

        Two pots read better in real life

        The two-pot version works best when you treat it like one mirrored cook: same ingredient order, same timing, different broth profiles.

        Do not overcook the shellfish

        Shrimp and scallops always go in last. Let the carryover heat do some of the finishing so they stay tender.

        Outdoor safety matters

        Set the burner on a stable surface, avoid overfilling the pot, and keep gloves plus a fire extinguisher nearby.
